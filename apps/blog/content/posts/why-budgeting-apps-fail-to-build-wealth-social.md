## LinkedIn Post

I spent a week reading the research on budgeting apps. The findings surprised me.

68% of users abandon their financial apps entirely. Finance apps retain just 4.2% of users by Day 30. And here's the kicker: peer-reviewed research shows that budgeters actually overspend their budgeted amounts by 1.3-1.4x compared to non-budgeters.

The mechanism? When you don't track spending precisely, uncertainty acts as a natural brake. A budget removes that uncertainty — you know you have $200 left in "dining out," so you spend it. The budget becomes a spending license, not a spending limit.

Meanwhile, housing and transportation alone account for 50.4% of all household spending. Job switchers see 10-15% salary increases vs 5% for those who stay. One 12% raise on a $70K salary = $8,400/year — the equivalent of cutting $700/month from your budget.

The math is clear: wealth is built by automating savings, growing income, and making a few big decisions well — not by categorizing every coffee.
